# 08 — UI

The player interface is a heads-up display over the world map (docs/07). The
first client (`src/client`, target `geosim`) implements the visual style,
renderer, top bar, agent bars and alert feed described here, over the world
map (docs/07: oblique Mercator, terrain, political fill and borders) and the
action tabs (*Action tabs* below); the inspector and the drawer tabs are still
to come.

## Visual style

These principles replace the earlier "statistics office" look. Inspiration:
Cities: Skylines 2.

- **Everything is a rectangle.** Square corners, never rounded. Panels,
  buttons, separators, glyphs and the map are all instanced quads.
- **Two kinds of panels:**
  - **Opaque** — flat, solid squares without a border for the few elements
    that must always be visible and readable (the top bar).
  - **Translucent** — frosted glass for anything that can be dismissed or
    ignored (agent bars, alerts, tooltips, future inspector/drawer). They
    Gaussian-blur what is behind them, dim and desaturate it slightly
    (×0.9, 18 %) and lay a **light cool-blue tint** over it at only 20 %
    strength (`glass_tint` #6E9FD8), with a faint sheen towards the top
    edge: the map clearly shows through, like frosted glass.
- **Docked vs floating:**
  - **Docked** panes sit **flush**, with no margin: the top bar at the
    window's top-left corner (x = 0, y = 0), the agent bars right under it,
    stacked without gaps at x = 0 (`panel_kind::glass_flush`, no edge). They
    read as one continuous pane of glass. Where two panes share an edge
    (top bar / first bar, bar / bar), a **1 px white strip** at 55 % opacity
    (`glass_separator`, scaled with the UI) separates them, over the shared
    width only; there are no margins between them.
  - **Floating** cards keep their **margins** (6 pt from the window edge and
    between cards) and a faint white edge (24 %): the alert feed, its header
    and the tooltips.
- **Text on glass** gets a faint dark backing: a 1 px drop shadow of the
  glyphs (`text_backing`, #08121F at 42 % × the text alpha), so white text
  stays legible over bright parts of the map. Text on the opaque bar has
  none.
- **Colour scheme:** soft blues (`src/client/style.hpp`). White text, white
  monochrome emoji used as icons. **Red text = bad, green text = good**
  for the player, used only on indicators and alerts; neutral values stay white.
- **Icons are emoji**, not images: no icon code, no texture other than the
  glyph atlas. Flags are the nation's emoji flag.
- **Typeface:** Source Sans 3 (variable font, any weight on the `wght` axis;
  the HUD uses 400 and 600). Sizes 12 / 14 / 15 pt at 1× scale.
- **Scaling:** UI scale = SDL3 window display scale (pixel density × content
  scale) × `--ui-scale`; layout metrics are points multiplied by that scale.
- **Numbers carry units** and a hover tooltip with their definition.

## Main menu

The client starts at the main menu (`--menu 0|1`; runs with `--frames` or
`--nation` go straight into a session). Behind it the world map pans slowly
east along the equator, zoomed out (about 200° across), with clouds; no other
chrome. On the left: the title **geosim** in big white letters (Source Sans 3
at weight 900) and a column of flat blue buttons with white text:

- **New session** — builds the default scenario (world2020) and drops the
  player into the paused game controlling no one (no agent bars); a flat
  "Loading" box shows while the scenario builds (~10 s).
- **Load session** — a window listing the saves, newest first; click to load.
  "No saved sessions yet" when there are none.
- **Multiplayer** — a placeholder window ("Not available yet").
- **Settings** — music volume (−/+ in 10 % steps), music on/off, clouds
  on/off, fullscreen on/off; written at once to the settings file.
- **Quit**.

The menu plays wartime music led by an arp solo (docs/14 *Main menu*); entering
a session hands the music back to the game's mood logic (or the N override).

## Controlling agents

- **+** (a small glass button at the top left, under the top bar and the
  agent bars) opens the **Control** window: every controllable agent, one list
  per kind — governments (executives), central banks, boards (large firms per
  nation and sector), unions, militaries, governors (regions), rebels — with
  their counts, a search field (type to filter by name or ISO3; Backspace,
  Esc closes) and a scrolling list. Clicking an agent controls it: it gets
  its own agent bar ("United States Government", "Texas Governor", "Canada
  manufacturing board"...). Controlled rows are marked.
- Each agent bar ends with an **answer-requests switch** (📨 ✓, default on), an **AI
  switch** and a **✖** (stop controlling). With 📨 ✓, requests to the agent (docs/06,
  *Requests*: a central bank asking its government to restrain spending, a union asking
  for a higher minimum wage...) wait for the player: they are pinned at the top of the
  alert column in yellow, above every other alert and never filtered or scrolled away,
  with what accepting does, the days left and what the agent's AI will answer when the
  window closes, and **Accept** / **Decline** buttons. The outcome arrives as an alert;
  requests the AI answered for the player show as "Answered for you". With 📨 ✗ the
  agent's AI answers at once (no communication lag).
  - **AI ✓** (default): the agent's AI keeps deciding; the player acts as
    advisor / forcer of its levers (override modes advise / force).
  - **AI ✗**: full control. Every lever of the agent is directed
    (`override_mode::direct`) at its current choice (`last_choice`), so nothing
    moves until the player changes it. Switching back clears the overrides.
  - Both go through a job at the sim's sync point; stopping control hands the
    levers back to the AI.
- The controlled set is per agent: alerts are the player's ("mine") when they
  involve the nation of any controlled agent, and the wartime mood applies when
  any controlled agent's nation is at war. `--nation USA,CAN` starts a session
  with those nations' governments and central banks controlled.

## Internet

**🌐** (top bar) opens a browser on the in-game internet (`browser.cpp`, pages from
`sim/web.cpp`): fictional, procedurally generated, text-only websites (no images; ads are
taken as blocked, and the status bar counts them) that report on the simulated world. It
is immersion and a news source for players who dismiss alerts.

- **Chrome** in the early-2000s style: simple tabs (click to switch, + to open, ✖ to
  close; not draggable), back / forward / reload / home, an address bar (a domain goes
  there, anything else is searched) and a search box on the **default engine**: the one
  most used across the nations of the player's agents (controlling Russian agents gives
  the Russian-sphere engine), else the most used worldwide. The **bookmarks bar** holds
  the five most used sites worldwide by active users (walled-off webs included). The
  start page has the default engine's search box, the top sites, the engines, news from
  the player's nations and world headlines.
- **Sectors**: the global web; walled-off hubs with their own engine and social network
  (China; Russia with Belarus, Kazakhstan, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan, Armenia); and closed
  national intranets where press freedom and liberties are near zero (a DPRK-like state
  runs every site). The player can open any of them.
- **Sites**, each with a category and an owner (a government, a firm's board, or
  independent / community / non-profit): search engines and social networks per sector;
  per nation a state portal, one to three newspapers named after the nation ("The
  Austrian Times"), dailies for its largest regions, sports, business briefs, forums
  (where people speak freely; state-run halls in closed nations) and tabloids
  (sensational in a free press, propaganda sheets in unfree ones); watchdogs hosted in
  the largest free nations that report on unfree ones with the true figures behind the
  official ones; global sport, business and forum sites.
- **Audiences are derived**: internet users = population × social-media share / 0.84
  (DataReportal 2020); a hub's own engine and network gain with low media freedom and
  filtering (Baidu, Yandex); the state outlet's share of online news grows with
  information control (Reuters Institute DNR 2020; RSF); sport ~15 %, forums ~10 %,
  tabloids ~12 %, business ~5 % of the richer half.
- **Propaganda** follows press freedom, information control and capture: bad news at
  home is omitted or spun, good news invented, official statistics flattered, friends'
  troubles left out and rivals' played up; controlled engines remove watchdog results
  and controlled networks hide sensitive topics. **404s**: a site goes down for the week
  with a chance growing with its nation's (or region's) instability - low legitimacy,
  unrest, protest, occupation, war, cyber damage, default - and, for state portals,
  incompetence (low state capacity also leaves them stale and "under construction").
- **People** (bylines, spokespeople, forum and social handles, goal scorers) are drawn
  from `assets/defs/names.csv` by culture, weighted by the current population of each
  identity group (`name_cultures.csv`): a USA made of Lithuanians has Lithuanian names.
  **Domains** follow `assets/defs/tlds.csv` (www.gov.uk, papers on .co.uk, watchdogs on
  .org.uk; nations without a ccTLD use .com.<iso3>). Nations show their common English
  name (`assets/defs/nation_names.csv`: South Korea, DR Congo) over the World Bank's.
- **Social feeds**: posts by the network's users, drawn from the nations where it is used by
  their internet users (a Russian-sphere network has Russian, Kazakh and Armenian posters),
  each with a default profile picture, its nation's flag and a username. How people write
  follows how free the network is - the stricter of its host's and the poster's press
  freedom × (1 − ½ information control): free networks are noisy, moral-emotional and
  emoji-laden (Brady et al. 2017; Vosoughi, Roy & Aral 2018), with absurd posts and stiff
  formal essays; controlled ones mostly cheerlead (King, Pan & Roberts 2013, 2017) and never
  carry bad news about the poster's home. Usernames: playful handles where speech is free
  ("lemoneater4735"), real names, titled real names ("Dr. Liam Peterson") or role numbers
  ("student7452") under control (real-name registration; self-censorship, Roberts 2018).
  Post templates, handle words, titles and torrent titles are in `assets/defs/web_text.csv`.
- **Markets and companies**: the largest firms (one to three per nation) have sites under
  generated names (`sim::web::firm_name`: a place, the nation or a founder's surname, a
  sector word and a suffix - "Karimi Agro", "Herat Construction Group"; the same names label
  boards across the UI); a stock exchange per nation (its largest city's; state-run where
  liberties are weak or state banks dominate) shows the index (1000 × the equity price) and
  the listed firms' weights; central banks publish their currency against the majors (%
  change since the start - the model has no levels).
- **Piracy** (`sim::web::piracy_of`): the share of software and media used unlicensed rises
  with the black market (informality) and falls with the rule of law and income (Marron &
  Steel 2000; Andrés 2006), calibrated to the BSA Global Software Survey 2018 within ~15
  points. Torrent trackers are hosted by the four laxest hosts (a large pirate audience,
  weak enforcement, enough bandwidth: RuTracker) and the two strongest free-speech havens
  (press freedom², infrastructure, a weak rights lobby: The Pirate Bay in Sweden); they go
  404 with their host's instability, enforcement (less for havens) and sanctions from
  rights holders' governments. Anti-piracy groups in the six most copyright-strong nations
  (enforcement × the economy's weight; Sell 2003), run by their largest business-services
  firm, list the trackers they pursue and the highest piracy rates.

## Action tabs

The tabs act for the **acting agent**: one of the controlled agents, the first
by default. Clicking an agent bar selects it (a blue strip marks it when there
are several), and each window has an "Acting as" row of the controlled agents.
Each tab shows only the levers that agent's kind owns (`sim/levers.hpp`
`lever_table`; NPI levers first, one per OxCGRT indicator).

| Tab | Content |
|---|---|
| 🏛 **Policies** | executive: the NPI levers and `lever_tab::policies`; central bank: *Monetary policy* (`monetary`); board: *Firm policy* (`firm`); union: *Wage bargaining* (`labour`); governor: *Regional policy* (`regional`). Militaries and rebels have none (a note says so). |
| 💰 **Fiscal policy** | budget tiles (revenue, spending, deficit / surplus, debt with its start, 10-year yield and spread) and the executive's `fiscal` levers. Revenue = tax flows (households, indirect, firms, resources, tariffs, carbon, wealth) over the run; deficit = net lending (change in `bal_gov`); spending = revenue − net lending, all % of nominal GDP over the same days; debt = −`bal_gov` / annualised nominal GDP. |
| 📦 **Trade** | tiles (exports and imports % of GDP with the change since the start, tariff, sanctions by / on), three flat pies — exports by partner (`bilateral_exports`), imports by partner (the partners' bilateral exports to this nation), exports by sector (`comp_x`), each top 8 + Other with a legend — the sanction lists and the tariff and capital-controls levers. |
| 🤝 **Diplomacy** | subtabs per counterpart kind (Governments, Central banks, Firms, Unions, Militaries, Governors, Rebels) and Organisations; a scrolling counterpart list on the left, the options toward the selected one on the right. |
| ⚔ **Military** | loyalty, defence spending, power and CINC, threat, nuclear; wars, disputes and allies; the defence lever. |
| 🗳 **Politics** | right half: the composition of power (below). |

**Lever rows**: name (a **bill** tag where it must pass the legislature), the
value now (`last_choice`), what the AI alone would choose (`last_ai_choice`),
a −/+ crank and a slider (click or drag; step and range from the table) for
the player's target, the unit and the mode:

- AI on: **Advise** (a strong prior the AI weighs) or **Force** (beyond the legal
  limits and the legislature, at a political cost: a red warning line shows
  while any lever is forced); neither = the AI alone. Cranking a lever with
  no mode starts advising it.
- AI off: **Direct** (set for every lever by the AI switch); cranks move the
  directed value.

**Composition of power** (Politics, right half):

- democracies (competitive, an electoral system, electoral index ≥ 0.5): a
  parliament diagram, one dot per representative on semicircular rows
  (`parliament.hpp`; the real lower-chamber size for the larger democracies,
  else the cube-root law of assembly size), parties in wedges left to right by
  economic position in the electoral map's party colours, government parties'
  dots ringed, the seat count and the government's share in the middle and a
  tooltip per party; then the party list (vote, seats, government ✓,
  positions on the three value axes);
- autocracies: the figures — executive (order ideal, fiscal hawk, rigidity ρ,
  legitimacy, approval, goal), military (loyalty, ideals, defence), central
  bank (independence, hawkishness, rate), union federation, the three largest
  boards (output share, hoarding, investment), active rebels;
- when the acting agent is a board, its board and firm cards come first;
- always, at the bottom: **higher echelon leanings**, a dot plot on each value
  axis (−1..1) of the top 10 % and top 25 % of the population by income per
  person against everyone.

**Diplomacy options** come from `sim::available_actions(actor, nation,
organisation, target_agent)`, which lists exactly the options toward the
selected counterpart: Governments → sanctions, disputes, war and peace, aid,
treaties (for an executive); toward one's own government, lobbying or pressing
(boards, unions, central banks, militaries, governors), a general strike
(unions), a coup (militaries), autonomy (governors), a settlement or foreign
backing (rebels). The agent subtabs (Central banks, Firms, Unions, Militaries,
Governors, Rebels) list the interactions between agent kinds of docs/06
(*Between agents*): pressing on one of the counterpart's levers, swap lines,
rescues, pacts, strikes, coup-proofing, counter-insurgency, power sharing and
more. Organisations → apply / leave, the climate pledge, complaints to rights
bodies (unions, rebels). Unavailable options are greyed with the reason as
tooltip; values are set beside the button (sanction level 0–1, aid % of GDP,
pledge USD/t, the treaty kind, the pressed lever and value, or an amount the
sim describes). Pairs with no channel say so. The result (accepted and
the sim's message) shows under the options and as an alert.

**Commands**: every player → sim write — lever overrides, the AI switch and
actions — is a `sim::command` (tag + payload union, `sim/commands.hpp`) pushed
by the UI thread into an SPSC queue, stamped with the current tick and an
increasing seq; the sim thread drains the due ones at its sync point before
each tick (also while paused) and answers through a result queue, matched by
seq (`sim_runner::pump`, `take_result`). A full queue keeps commands in a
backlog retried each frame. Read-only snapshots (`request_tab`, every 300 ms
while a tab is open) still run as sync-point jobs.

## Settings and saves

- **Settings**: `$XDG_CONFIG_HOME/geosim/settings.txt` (default
  `~/.config/geosim/settings.txt`), `key = value` lines: `music_volume`,
  `music`, `clouds`, `fullscreen`. Read at start, before the command line
  (which overrides it); written by the Settings window.
- **Saves**: **Ctrl+S** in a session writes `sim::save_game` at the next sync
  point to `$XDG_DATA_HOME/geosim/saves/<scenario>-<YYYYMMDD-HHMMSS>.gsave`
  (default `~/.local/share/geosim/saves`); the Load session window lists that
  directory; the scenario (for names) comes from the file name. The
  controlled agents are not part of the save (re-add them with +).

```
├──────────────────────────────────────────────────────┬──────────────────────────────────┤
│ 🇺🇸 🏛 United States Government  Support 46.6%  …      │            ┌ ⚔ War: RUS – UKR  ✖ ┐ │  alert cards (right column,
├───────────────────────────────────────────┬──────────┘            │   Thu 24 Feb 2022    │ │  glass, wheel scrolls)

└───────────────────────────────────────────┘                       │   Louisiana · …      │ │
                                                                     │   12 dead · $19 bn …  │ │
   (map fills the window behind; a window — Politics, Diplomacy…     │ 1–9 of 40  Dismiss all│ │
    — fills the space left of the alerts, below the first agent bar) └───────────────────────┘ │

                                                                     │ ■ Flood: now  …      │ │  the alerts end above it)
                                                                     └──────────────────────┘ │
```

- **Top bar** (flush with the top edge, opaque, full width): the current
  date, SimCity 4-style time controls (pause and
  speeds 1–5), one button per action tab (Policies, Fiscal policy, Trade,
  Diplomacy, Military, Politics) and one per map view (political, climate, economy,
  trade, energy, epidemic, unrest; exactly one active); at its right end the
  alert category filters and the notification bell. It shows no flag or nation: the
  player may control several nations, and each agent bar names its own.
- **Agent bars** (directly below, flush, translucent, separated by white
  strips): one per agent the player controls — for now each controlled
  nation's executive and central bank, in `--nation` order. Each bar starts
  with its **nation's flag** emoji and the agent's icon, then a label that
  names the nation: "United States Government", "United States Central
  Bank", "Canada Government" (the scenario's `nations.csv` name, cut at a
  comma: "Korea, Rep." → "Korea"; the ISO3 code if there is none). Then the
  indicators, coloured green / red when good / bad for the player. An alert
  is the player's ("mine") when it involves any controlled nation.
- **Alerts** (right column, translucent floating cards with margins): journal events (wars,
  peace, coups, regime changes, elections, defaults, insurgencies…
  worldwide; disputes, agreements, no-confidence votes, court rulings only for
  the player's nation), newest first, each dismissable.
- **Alerts sit on the right side** of the window, below the top bar, above the
  map legend: cards only, no header. A small footer shows the position in the
  list ("1–9 of 40") and an unobtrusive **Dismiss all**.
- **Notification bell** (🔔, right end of the top bar): a red badge counts the
  alerts added since it was last clicked (capped at "99+"); clicking it hides
  or shows the list and marks everything seen.
- **Category filters** (icons left of the bell; each toggles, active ones are
  highlighted, with a small count of the listed alerts in that category).
  Local notices (scenario loading / ready) are never filtered.

  | Icon | Category | Alerts |
  |---|---|---|
  | 📈 | economy | sovereign defaults, shipping chokepoints |
  | 🏛 | politics | elections, regime changes, no-confidence votes, court rulings, term limits |
  | ⚔ | security | wars, coups, rebel movements, cyber attacks |
  | 🤝 | diplomacy | peace, sanctions, disputes, concessions, alliance calls, agreements |
  | 🌀 | disasters | cyclones, floods, droughts, earthquakes, tsunamis (disaster records) |
  | 🦠 | health | new pathogen variants |
  | ✊ | society | insurgencies |
- **Map modes are not tabs**: selecting one (political, climate, economy,
  trade, energy, epidemic, unrest) only recolours the map. Its legend sits in
  a compact flat box (white, blue title strip) in the bottom-right corner; the
  alert column ends above it with the usual gap, so the list shows fewer
  alerts (and scrolls) rather than being covered.
- **Windows**: the action buttons open a window: flat white with a blue title
  strip and a ✖, filling the space below the top bar and the acting agent's bar
  (only that bar shows while a window is open), from the left margin to the
  alert column, down to the bottom margin — the same 6 pt margins the alerts
  keep. See *Action tabs*.
- **Politics window** (🗳), split in two halves:
  - *left*: the electoral map of one nation — its real region shapes in plain
    Mercator with atlas-style insets for distant landmasses (docs/07 *Electoral
    map*), with a row of flat mode buttons (winner, each party, population,
    turnout; for non-democracies regime support, unrest, insurgency,
    population), ◀ ▶ to step through nations (or click a nation on the map)
    and the national result below; its tooltips as before;
  - *right*: the composition of power (see *Action tabs*).
- The **top bar's** opaque blue strip spans the full window width (its
  controls stay at the left).
- **Map tooltips** (flat style, like the windows): pointing at a region (or
  an electoral-map region) shows its name, its controller and the view's
  headline indicator. Keeping the pointer still (within 4 px) for **0.75 s of
  real time** (works while paused) expands it into the detailed panel
  (docs/07 *Views*); a thin blue bar under its title strip fills meanwhile.
  The expanded panel stays open while the pointer is on it; pointing at
  another region collapses it and restarts the timer. Other tooltips and
  translucent elements keep the glass style.
- **Disaster alerts** (`hazard_feed`, one per sim `disaster` record, updated
  in place as its toll grows): the kind's emoji (🌀 cyclone, 🌧 flood,
  🏜 drought, 🏚 earthquake, 🌊 tsunami), the title from
  `sim::describe_disaster` ("Hurricane Laura", "Magnitude 7.8 earthquake"),
  "region, nation · date" (region names from the scenario's `regions.csv`,
  matched to sim regions by owner and coordinates; nation names from
  `nations.csv`), and a third line with the impact: deaths, people affected,
  damage in USD and as % of GDP. A forecast (an approaching cyclone, status
  *warning*) reads "Could cause …"; its landfall becomes a second alert. Red
  when the hit nation is one the player controls (always shown); elsewhere
  only notable events (≥ 50 deaths, ≥ 1 M affected or ≥ USD 1 bn). These
  replace the journal's "🌪 Disaster in X" alert.

## Interaction patterns

- **Hover** any number → tooltip with its definition (implemented for the
  agent-bar indicators and the top-bar buttons).
- **Keyboard:** Esc closes the open window, Ctrl+S saves the session,
  Space / P toggles pause (resumes the last speed), 0 pauses,
  1–5 pick a speed, + / − step the speed, M mutes / unmutes the music,
  B skips to the next song, N cycles the music mood (auto → calm → tense →
  crisis → triumph → wartime → auto; a toast shows the choice),
  Ctrl+Q quits. Every action will be
  reachable by keyboard; a command palette (`Ctrl+K`) is planned.
- **Mouse:** click buttons (press and release inside), wheel scrolls the
  alert list. On the map (pointer not over a panel, `ui_context::wants_mouse()`):
  drag pans, the wheel zooms at the pointer. Arrow keys pan and Page Up /
  Page Down zoom from the keyboard. **C** toggles the cloud layer (climate view).
- Planned: click a number → Trace tab; Shift+click regions → comparisons in
  Charts; the override flow (inspector → agent → *Advise / Direct / Force* →
  policy editor → *Commit*); sortable tables with copy as TSV.

The UI is **immediate-mode**: the HUD is rebuilt from the simulation every
frame; only the pressed button, scroll offset and alert list persist.
Dear ImGui may be used **only** for developer overlays behind a compile-time
option; it does not ship in the player UI.

### Rendering

Vulkan 1.3 with dynamic rendering and synchronization2 (no render passes).
Every element is one instance of a single instanced-quad vertex format
(`quad_instance`, 48 bytes: rect, uv / material parameters, colour, border
colour, border width, flags); the vertex shader expands 6 vertices from
`gl_VertexIndex`, so there is no vertex buffer besides the instance buffer.

- **Materials** are pipelines sharing that vertex shader and instance format,
  each with its own fragment shader: `ui` (flat, frosted, glyph, scene-copy and
  arc modes selected by `flags`; *arc* draws an antialiased disc sector or ring
  inscribed in the quad — start / end angle in turns, inner radius, edge gap in
  `u0..v1` — for the pies and the hemicycle) and `map_placeholder` (shown until the world map
  is loaded) and `flag` (a national flag, below). The world map itself is not a quad material: `map_renderer`
  draws a fullscreen triangle (`fullscreen.vert` + `world_map.frag`) with its
  own pipeline layout (terrain, ID raster, region-table storage buffer). A future chart is a new
  material: add `shaders/chart_x.frag`, an entry in `material` and in the
  material table in `ui_renderer.cpp`, and push one quad whose `u0..v1` carry
  its parameters. Consecutive instances of the same material are one draw.
- **Frame:**
  1. *Scene pass* into an offscreen image: the world map first, then the
     opaque layer (top bar and its text).
  2. *Blur:* scene → ½ → ¼ resolution (bilinear 2×2 box per step), then a
     separable Gaussian (σ = 3 texels at ¼ resolution ≈ 12 px, 15 taps)
     horizontal and vertical.
  3. *Final pass* on the swapchain: a copy of the scene, then the overlay
     layer: frosted panels sample the blurred image under their rect, dim
     and desaturate it slightly, mix in their tint (tint alpha = strength)
     and add a top-edge sheen (`ui.frag`); then the separator strips (solid
     quads), text (glyphs with their 1 px dark backing first) and tooltips.
  Translucent panels blur the scene only, not other translucent panels
  overlapping them (as in C:S2, panels rarely overlap).
- The UI works in display (gamma) space on a UNORM swapchain, so the colours
  in `style.hpp` are the sRGB values on screen.
- Validation: `--validation 1` or `GEOSIM_VK_VALIDATION=1` enables
  `VK_LAYER_KHRONOS_validation`; the message count is printed at exit and
  automated runs (`--frames`) exit with code 3 on validation errors.

### Text

- **Fonts** (`assets/fonts`, all SIL OFL 1.1, licences alongside):
  Source Sans 3 (`SourceSans3-Variable.ttf`, google/fonts) and Noto Emoji
  (`NotoEmoji-Variable.ttf`, the monochrome Noto emoji, google/fonts).
- **Rasterisation:** FreeType into one 1024² R8 coverage atlas (shelf packer,
  glyphs cached per face, size and glyph; cleared on display-scale change or
  when full). Both fonts are variable: one FT_Face per (family, weight) is
  instantiated from the in-memory file with `FT_Set_Var_Design_Coordinates`
  on `wght`, so any weight can be requested (`font_weight{550}`).
- **Shaping-lite:** UTF-8 → codepoints; per-codepoint fallback (emoji font
  first for pictographs and symbols, then the text font); horizontal advances;
  variation selectors and ZWJ skipped; regional-indicator pairs → the emoji
  font's GSUB ligature (lookup type 4, parsed directly). National flags are
  not emoji: a private-use codepoint (U+F0000 + flag index, `flag_set::token`)
  becomes a flag quad ([Flags](#flags)). No kerning, ligatures, complex scripts or bidi yet. HarfBuzz is not
  installed on the dev machine; when CMake finds it, it is linked and
  `GEOSIM_HAS_HARFBUZZ` is defined so `text_system::shape` can be replaced
  without touching callers (needed for localisation / RTL).
- **Emoji are monochrome** (white, tinted like text). Colour emoji
  (CBDT / COLR) are not supported: they would need an RGBA atlas.

### Flags

National flags are data, drawn on the GPU — no images, no emoji, no SVG — so a flag can be
changed in game (by the player or an agent; not yet exposed) by editing its elements.

- **Data:** `assets/defs/flags.csv` lists each nation's flag (the one it uses at the UN, or its
  internationally recognised one) as simplified vexillological elements in painter's order:
  `flag W H` (official proportions: Switzerland 1:1, the United States 19:10, Qatar 28:11),
  field, horizontal / vertical stripes with weights, rectangles, polygons (triangles,
  chevrons, serrations), bands (`line`: diagonals, saltires), discs and rings, stars,
  crescents, star rings and grids, `inset` (another flag in a canton: the Union Jack) and
  `emblem`s. `assets/defs/flag_emblems.csv` is the shared pool of low-poly charges in a
  [-1, 1] box (shield, eagles, crown, wreath, trident, maple leaf, cedar, ...; `mirror` parts
  are symmetric shapes given by their left half); a coat of arms is a few stacked emblems
  (Serbia: a red shield, a white double-headed eagle, a gold crown). Designs keep what makes
  a flag recognisable at text size, as in Twemoji, not the full arms; colours follow the
  national specifications (Chad's and Romania's yellows differ).
- **Compile:** `core/flags.cpp` parses both files and lowers every element to primitives in flag
  units (height 1): polygons (vertices), discs / rings and crescents, each with a colour and
  bounds. Stripes become bands that run to the far edge so the next one paints over them (no
  seams); emblems are transformed (centre, size, rotation, stretch / mirror); insets recurse.
- **Draw:** the `flag` material (`shaders/flag.frag`) reads the primitives and vertices from two
  storage buffers (descriptor bindings 3 and 4 of the UI set, uploaded when the book's revision
  changes) and evaluates a flag's range per pixel: signed distance per primitive (polygons by
  the even-odd rule), one-pixel antialiasing, painter's compositing, a faint inner edge so pale
  flags keep an outline on light pages. One quad per flag; its `u0, v0` are the primitive range.
- **In text:** a flag occupies a universal cell — 3:2, about the cap height (0.68 em) — and
  is fitted inside it with its own proportions, centred: a square Swiss flag gets side margins,
  a 19:10 US flag fills the width. `sim_runner::nation_label` puts the flag before a nation's
  name (agent bars, map tooltips, the tabs' nation lists). `ui_context::flag` draws one at any
  size (up to ~256 px).
- **Check:** `geosim --flag-sheet N` shows page N of every flag (100 + N: three times larger),
  each also inline in text; `tests/test_flags.cpp` checks the parser, the lowering and that
  every scenario nation has a flag.

### Simulation data

The client links `geosim_sim`, builds the scenario (`--scenario`, default
`world2020`; or loads a save) on a dedicated simulation thread and advances days at the selected
speed. The simulation never waits for the UI:

- **Scalars** (the tick, nation floats) are read by the UI thread directly
  from the DCON SoA while the simulation writes them. Values may be stale or
  from different days; ids are bounded by the SoA, so a stale id reads stale
  data, never out of bounds. This is accepted for display.
- **Lists** (anything whose storage can move) are requested with a
  `std::future`: the job runs on the simulation thread at its sync point
  between two days. The alert feed requests journal entries since the last
  scanned tick at most every 250 ms (`sim_runner::request_journal`).
- **Indicators** of each controlled agent (see *Controlling agents*; other
  kinds than the two below show a few values of what they act on: unions
  unemployment / real wage / density, militaries loyalty / defence, boards
  hoarding / leverage / GDP, governors regional unrest / grievance / trust,
  rebels strength / demand):
  Government — incumbent's vote share, real GDP growth (first year: run mean
  vs 2019, as `sim::summarize`; then trailing 12 months vs the year before,
  from the `acc_*` accumulators), unemployment, change in the budget balance
  (% of GDP, from day 30), days to the election; Central bank — CPI inflation
  (same window as GDP), policy rate, 10-year yield, real exchange rate.
  Good / bad thresholds are in `sim_runner.cpp`.

This replaces, for now, the planned sim *snapshot*
([09](09-engine-architecture.md#threading)); writes go through the command
queue (*Action tabs*, Commands).

### Build and run

```
cmake -S . -B build/client -DGEOSIM_BUILD_CLIENT=ON
cmake --build build/client --target geosim
build/client/src/geosim [--nation USA[,CAN...]] [--speed 0..5] [--ui-scale 1.5]
```

Needs SDL3, Vulkan 1.3 (loader, headers, `glslangValidator`), FreeType and
zlib. The map asset is tracked; to rebuild it see [07](07-map-and-filters.md#data).
`--map-view LON,LAT,SPAN` sets the initial view (headless screenshots),
`--clouds 0` hides the clouds, `--hazard-demo 1` draws demo cyclones and an
earthquake;
automated runs (`--frames`) also wait for the map to load.
Headless check (no display, e.g. with Mesa lavapipe):

```
SDL_VIDEODRIVER=offscreen build/client/src/geosim --validation 1 \
    --frames 60 --speed 5 --until-day 200 --screenshot out.ppm \
    --inject "30:click:226,25;60:resize:1100,700"
```

Music: `--music 0` disables it, `--music-volume 0..1`, `--music-seed N`;
without an audio device the client logs a warning and runs silent.
`geosim --render-music out.wav --seed 7 --mood calm` renders a song
offline (docs/14-audio.md).
