# World commodity markets besides crude oil (hand-written; moddable). Crude oil keeps
# its own market in src/sim/systems/markets.cpp (OPEC+ decisions); these markets run in
# src/sim/systems/commodities.cpp with the same stock-flow price rule:
#     target = price0 * exp(-flow_sensitivity * (supply - demand)/demand0
#                           - inventory_sensitivity * (stocks - normal)/(demand0 * inventory_days))
# Prices are indexes (1 = 2019 average). Producer and consumer shares per nation are in
# assets/scenario/<year>/commodities_curated.csv (world2020: gas and coal also in the
# generated commodities.csv). Keys are at most 4 characters; 'oil' is reserved.
#
#  world_value_bn      2019 world production value, USD bn = 2019 world output x 2019 average
#                      price, rounded. Quantities: FAOSTAT 2019 (crops, livestock, forestry),
#                      USDA FAS PSD 2019/20 (stocks, use), ICO / ICCO crop year 2018/19,
#                      USGS Mineral Commodity Summaries 2020 (MCS; minerals, 2019 estimates),
#                      World Nuclear Association (uranium), BP Statistical Review 2020 (gas,
#                      coal). Prices: World Bank Commodity Price Data ("Pink Sheet") 2019
#                      annual averages unless noted.
#  extractive{1}      a point-source resource (energy, ores, mined minerals): its production value
#                      counts as resource rents (geopolitics.cpp war incentive; Collier & Hoeffler
#                      2004 on lootable resources, Ross 2012 on rentier states). Crops, livestock,
#                      timber and manufactured fertiliser are not rents.
#  demand_elasticity   short-run price elasticity of demand
#  supply_elasticity   short-run price elasticity of supply
#  inventory_days      normal stocks in days of demand (USDA PSD ending stocks / use for crops)
#  harvest_sd          quarterly s.d. of the supply shock (weather; crops only)
#  biofuel_share       demand share that tracks the crude price (ethanol, biodiesel)
#  income_elasticity   demand response to household income (Engel's law: staples low)
#  weights{ sector{w} }  demand follows the activity of these sectors; without weights,
#                      demand follows population and income.
#
# Food: wheat, maize and rice replace the former aggregate 'food' (grains, 550 bn) and iron
# ore and copper replace 'met' (340 bn); the domestic food price (commodities.cpp) follows a
# nation's own consumption-weighted index of the staple markets (whea, maiz, rice, sugr,
# palm) and still accepts a legacy 'food' market if a mod defines one.
# Elasticities, general: grains - Roberts & Schlenker 2013 (AER; caloric demand -0.05 to
# -0.08, supply ~0.1); metals - Fally & Sayre 2018 (NBER WP 24965; short-run elasticities of
# primary commodities near zero); energy - Labandeira, Labeaga & Lopez-Otero 2017.

           weights{ ene{0.45} man{0.35} csv{0.10} gov{0.10} } }
